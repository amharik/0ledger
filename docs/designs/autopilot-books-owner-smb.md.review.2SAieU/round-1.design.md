## Problem Statement

Owner-run Indian SMBs do their own books manually in Excel or accounting software, or leave them until a deadline. They need clean, filing-safe books without learning double-entry or hiring a full-time accountant.

0ledger already captures email and statements and produces a Tally-compatible ledger. Today it still asks the owner to act like a bookkeeper. The product needs to move from "here is a ledger, enter data" to "here are the few things that need your judgment."

## Demand Evidence

None outside this session.

- No named pilot owner.
- No payment.
- No repeated use.
- No one who would scramble if the product disappeared.

This is the biggest risk in the plan. The first milestone is validation, not code. The assignment at the end of this document is a 5-owner concierge close.

## Status Quo

The owner manually enters transactions into books or Excel, or does nothing until GST, TDS, ITR, a loan application, or a CA request forces action.

Receipts live in WhatsApp, email, and bank portals. Bank statements are downloaded by hand. A part-time bookkeeper or CA cleans up the mess at a deadline.

The cost is hours per month, missed tax set-asides, CA cleanup fees, and no reliable cash view.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

The actual human is the owner/founder of a 5 to 50 person Indian SMB, roughly INR 1 to 50 crore revenue, who does their own books or supervises one part-time person. They use WhatsApp, email, and bank portals daily. They file GST and hand off to a CA.

The narrowest wedge is a monthly close autopilot:

1. The owner supplies a bank statement and email attachments.
2. The system proposes postings with confidence.
3. The owner reviews only exceptions.
4. The CA receives a clean Tally and GST-ready pack.

The owner does not have to learn double-entry. The CA does not have to re-key the books.

## Why Now

India has a large MSME base but a much smaller active GST-paying market. Tally remains dominant in the mid-market, and CAs remain the trust channel. The 2026 AI finance market is moving from processing every transaction to reviewing exceptions. AI-native accounting products are raising money on that promise.

UPI, WhatsApp, and e-invoicing have made the money trail digital, but owners still cannot turn that trail into books without manual work. The bottleneck is conversion and trust, not data availability.

## 100x Thesis

100x does not mean 100x more features. It means 100x less owner effort per month and 100x faster time to clean, filing-safe books.

The product becomes a close assistant, not a ledger. The owner sees cash position, tax set-aside, missing documents, and a short exception queue. The ledger, reports, and Tally export are byproducts for the CA.

## Constraints

- Local-first is the trust promise. Raw bank data, email, and documents stay on the owner's machine by default.
- Phase 1 is Chromium MV3, no backend, and no remote code.
- Google Drive and Gmail use restricted scopes and require verification for public distribution.
- GST and TDS are capture-only today. No return filing or computation.
- OCR is not bundled. Scanned PDFs are rejected with a clear error.
- One company per workspace, INR-first, April to March financial year.
- Phase 1 does not add mobile, WhatsApp capture, email-in addresses, AI, or sync.
- Multi-surface capture and sync are Phase 2 decisions. They will require an end-to-end encrypted relay or encrypted Drive sync, not a plaintext 0ledger server.

## Premises

1. The owner's real job is knowing cash position, tax set-aside, and filing readiness. Double-entry is a byproduct.
2. Pain spikes at GST, TDS, ITR, loan, and CA handoff deadlines, not daily. The wedge must be deadline-triggered.
3. The 100x product is exception-only bookkeeping: capture automatically, auto-post with confidence, owner reviews exceptions, CA gets a clean pack.
4. Chromium local-first is a trust advantage for capture and a distribution disadvantage. It cannot be the only surface forever.
5. The CA is not the primary user, but is the trust anchor and distribution channel.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Close Inbox

A smaller exception-review workflow on top of the existing extension. Effort M, risk Med, completeness 6/10. Reuses ingestion, reconciliation, reports, and Tally export.

Rejected as the final plan because it stays desktop-first and does not deliver the multi-surface 100x outcome. It remains the fallback if Autopilot Books proves too broad.

### Approach B: Autopilot Books

The chosen approach. A money-event inbox with owner exception review, close cockpit, CA handoff, and a staged path to multi-surface capture, AI, and sync.

Effort XL overall, risk High, completeness 10/10. Phase 1 is deliberately narrower.

### Approach C: CA-Ready Cleanup Sprint

A deadline-triggered cleanup service sold through CA firms. Effort S/M, risk Low/Med, completeness 4/10. Shortest path to revenue, but low daily engagement and a weaker moat.

Rejected as the main plan because it does not create the owner habit or the 100x daily experience.

## Recommended Approach: Autopilot Books, Phase 1

### Product shape

Phase 1 is a local-only close assistant inside the existing extension.

- **Capture:** bank statement import and email attachment import through the existing ingestion paths.
- **Normalize:** convert each statement line or document into a `MoneyEvent` with source, amount, date, party, reference, tax hints, attachment, and confidence.
- **Classify:** deterministic rules first. No AI in Phase 1. Every suggestion carries evidence and confidence.
- **Post:** a `MoneyEvent` becomes a double-entry voucher only after validation. High-confidence events can auto-post if the owner enables it. Low-confidence events go to the exception queue.
- **Review:** the owner sees only unmatched, low-confidence, missing-document, and tax-sensitive items. One-tap approve, split, recategorize, or mark for CA.
- **Close:** a monthly close cockpit shows cash position, GST/TDS set-aside, missing documents, unreconciled lines, and a readiness score.
- **Handoff:** one-click Tally XML, GST summary, party outstanding, and missing-items pack for the CA. The pack is immutable and auditable.

### Architecture

```text
Bank statement / email attachment
        |
        v
Existing ingestion adapters
        |
        v
MoneyEvent normalizer
  source, amount, date, party, reference, attachment, confidence
        |
        v
Rule-based classification
  party match -> ledger match -> tax hint -> confidence
        |
        v
Ledger adapter
  validation -> voucher -> append-only audit
        |
        +--> Exception queue
        +--> Close cockpit
        +--> CA pack: Tally XML / GST summary / missing items
        |
        v
Local IndexedDB store + encrypted backup
```

No backend. No remote classifier. No new network calls in Phase 1.

### Data model

New records:

- `MoneyEvent`: id, workspaceId, source, sourceRef, date, amountMinor, direction, partyHint, narration, attachmentId, confidence, status.
- `ClassificationSuggestion`: moneyEventId, ledgerId, partyId, taxHint, confidence, reasons, ruleId.
- `CloseItem`: workspaceId, period, type, status, owner, dueDate, evidence.
- `CaPack`: workspaceId, period, generatedAt, manifest, voucherIds, readiness, missingItems.

Existing records stay authoritative for vouchers, ledgers, reports, and audit events.

### Rule engine

Phase 1 rules are data-driven and deterministic:

- Exact reference or UTR match.
- Amount plus date window plus party match.
- Narration keyword to ledger mapping.
- Bank charge, interest, GST, TDS, salary, rent, and common vendor patterns.
- Duplicate detection by source reference, amount, date, and content hash.

Every rule returns a confidence and a reason list. The exception queue sorts by risk, not by date.

### Exception queue

The owner sees a short list, not a ledger:

- Unmatched bank lines.
- Low-confidence classifications.
- Missing invoices or receipts.
- Tax-sensitive items.
- Duplicate candidates.
- Unbalanced or incomplete vouchers.

Each item has one primary action and an "ask CA" option. The queue is the daily surface. Reports are secondary.

### Close cockpit

The monthly close view answers four questions:

1. How much cash is available?
2. How much GST/TDS should be set aside?
3. What is missing before filing?
4. Is the CA pack ready?

The readiness score is computed from unreconciled lines, missing documents, and Tally readiness checks. It is a checklist, not a tax opinion.

### CA pack

The pack contains:

- Tally XML for vouchers and masters.
- Trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, party outstanding, and bill aging.
- GST/TDS capture summary.
- Missing-items list with owner notes.
- Manifest with counts, hashes, and generation time.

The CA acceptance test is explicit: three CA firms must accept the pack with no rework before the build proceeds beyond Phase 1.

### Build sequence

1. **Validation gate, no code:** 10 owner interviews, 5 concierge closes, 3 CA pack reviews. Measure time, exception rate, CA rework, and willingness to pay.
2. **MoneyEvent inbox:** add the model, repository, and adapters on top of the existing ingestion path.
3. **Rule engine and exception queue:** add deterministic classification, confidence, reasons, and the owner review surface.
4. **Close cockpit:** add the monthly checklist, cash and tax view, missing documents, and readiness score.
5. **CA pack:** add the Tally/GST pack, manifest, and CA acceptance test.
6. **Hardening:** tests for duplicates, unbalanced vouchers, restore, and large statements.

Phase 1 reuses:

- `src/core/ingest/` for bank statement and email parsing.
- `src/core/ledger/reconciliation.ts` for matching and confidence patterns.
- `src/core/ledger/reports.ts` for trial balance, P&L, and outstanding.
- `src/core/interchange/tally-export.ts` for the CA pack.
- `src/ui/StatementImport.tsx` and `src/ui/ReconciliationPanel.tsx` for import and review patterns.
- `src/core/backup/backup.ts` for encrypted backup.

### Phase 2 and Phase 3

Only after the validation gate passes:

- Phase 2: email-in address, mobile share sheet, OCR, WhatsApp Web capture, optional E2EE sync.
- Phase 3: AI classification, CA portal, multi-client CA workspace, filing integrations.

### AI policy

AI is not part of Phase 1. When it arrives:

- Rules first. AI is a fallback for ambiguous narration, party matching, and ledger mapping.
- AI never posts silently. Every suggestion includes evidence, confidence, and a model or rule identifier.
- High-confidence auto-post is opt-in per owner and per category.
- Low-confidence items always go to the owner or CA.
- On-device inference is preferred. Cloud AI is opt-in, with redaction and explicit disclosure.
- No bank credentials. No plaintext server-side ledger.

### Moat

- Indian bank, FASTag, UPI, and GST document adapters.
- Reconciliation rules and correction data from real owners.
- CA distribution and trust.
- Local-first privacy that generic cloud accounting tools cannot match.
- Tally-compatible handoff without forcing the owner into Tally.

## Risks and Kill Criteria

- **No demand:** fewer than 3 of 10 owners hand over last month's statements after the pilot. Kill or narrow.
- **Wrong buyer:** owners will not pay and CAs will not pay. Test both before build.
- **CA rework:** any CA rejects the pack as unusable. Fix the pack before adding features.
- **Capture friction:** owners will not import statements or email attachments. Stop and test a different capture surface.
- **Scope blowout:** backend, mobile, OCR, AI, and CA portal at once. Phase 1 forbids all of them.
- **Compliance risk:** the product gives wrong tax guidance. Keep it a pack and checklist, not a filing engine, until reviewed.
- **Privacy risk:** email attachments and bank data are sensitive. No new network calls in Phase 1.

## Open Questions

1. Who pays first: the owner at INR 499 to 999 per month, or the CA firm at INR 1,999 to 4,999 per month for a client book?
2. Which capture surface wins first after Phase 1: email-in, mobile share sheet, WhatsApp Web, or OCR?
3. Is on-device AI good enough for classification, or is opt-in cloud AI required?
4. Can the no-backend promise survive multi-surface sync, or is an E2EE relay acceptable?
5. Does the CA handoff stay a Tally/GST pack, or does the product need filing integrations?
6. What is the first named design partner, and who introduces them?

## Success Criteria

- Owner spends under 30 minutes per month on bookkeeping in Phase 1, down from 4 to 8 hours. Target under 15 minutes after Phase 2.
- At least 80% of transactions are captured automatically.
- At least 70% of captured transactions are auto-coded with acceptable confidence.
- Fewer than 20% of transactions need owner review.
- 5 of 10 pilot owners hand over statements without hand-holding.
- 3 of 10 say they would pay after the pilot; 1 actually pays or signs a paid pilot.
- 3 CA firms accept the pack without rework.
- Time from statement upload to clean books is under 24 hours.
- No duplicate postings, no silent data loss, no unbalanced voucher.

## Distribution Plan

- **CA-led:** recruit 10 CA firms, each with 20 to 50 SMB clients. Offer a white-label close pack and referral fee. This is the trust channel.
- **Owner-led:** Chrome Web Store listing and content for owners who do their own books. Do not rely on extension-store search.
- **Pricing:** free local core; INR 499 to 999 per month for autopilot and CA pack; CA seat pricing for multiple clients; paid cleanup sprint as a fallback.
- **Delivery:** existing GitHub Actions for typecheck, tests, and build. Add signed release, store listing, privacy policy, and a mobile PWA deploy only when Phase 2 starts.

## Dependencies

- Bank statement adapters and fixtures. Current adapters cover 10 banks and FASTag.
- Email attachment parsing. Existing MIME routing covers this.
- OCR for scanned PDFs. Not in Phase 1. Current build rejects them.
- Google OAuth verification if Gmail sync is part of the public product.
- CA design partners and 10 owner pilots.
- Privacy and security review before email-in, WhatsApp capture, or any relay.

## The Assignment

Do not write code this week. Run a 5-owner concierge close:

1. Recruit 5 owners who do their own books.
2. Ask each for last month's bank statement and 10 expense receipts from email.
3. Produce a clean Tally and GST pack using the current app plus manual work.
4. Time the work and record every exception.
5. Ask each owner: "Would you pay INR 499 per month for this to happen automatically?"
6. Ask their CA: "Would you accept this pack?"

Bring back the times, exception count, CA reaction, and payment answers. That evidence decides whether Phase 1 gets built.
